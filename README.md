# GearCraft

A gear and gear-train designer: a friendlier take on Gearotic, built with Rust and egui.

One window, no pop-up parameter dialogs:

- **Workspace** (top left): what you are designing, which sets the ribbon's tools. Spur Gears, Bevel
  Gears and Timing Pulleys today; internal/planetary, helical, clock wheels and escapements will join the list.
  Each workspace shows only its own parts: switching to Timing Pulleys puts the gears away, and
  switching back brings them (and what you had selected) back. One project file holds them all.
- **Train** (left): the gears and pulleys in the project as a tree. Each is either fixed, meshing
  with an earlier gear, belted to an earlier pulley, or sharing an earlier gear's axle (compound
  gears for clock and reduction trains).
- **Canvas** (centre): live drawing of the whole train. Scroll to zoom, drag empty space to pan,
  drag a fixed gear to move it, drag a meshing gear to swing it round its partner (Shift snaps to 15°).
  Space runs the animation. View → 3D shows the parts as solids of their face width, stacked along
  their shafts (Axial offset in the Placement section moves one along its shaft); drag to orbit.
- **Properties** (right): the selected gear's parameters, with derived dimensions, mesh data
  (centre distance, working pressure angle, contact ratio, clearance) and warnings updating as you type.

Tooth outlines are generated by simulating the rack cutter, so root fillets and undercut are exact.

Metric (module, mm) and inch (diametral pitch, inches) projects: pick with the Metric | Inch switch at the left of the toolbar
(it offers to convert existing gears to the nearest standard pitch), or File → New metric/inch project. Lengths,
the grid and exports follow the project's units. Tooth standards: ISO 53, AGMA full depth (20°/25°),
AGMA 14½° full depth, AGMA fine pitch (1.2/P + 0.002") and AGMA 20° stub.

## Bevel gears

Straight bevel gears with spherical involute teeth, the exact bevel counterpart of the involute,
so a pair has conjugate action along the whole face. New Pair places a 20-tooth pinion and a 40-tooth
wheel at right angles; Mesh Bevel (M) adds a mate to the selected gear at any shaft angle, and the pitch
cones follow from the tooth counts. A gear's first mate sets its cone; further mates fit that cone, so
a gear can drive several others. Proportions are the equal-addendum system at the large end (addendum
1 m, dedendum 1.25 m), with a warning when the face width passes a third of the cone distance or the
virtual tooth count is small enough to undercut. Bevel gears show in 3D only and export as STEP.

## Hypoid and spiral bevel gears

Hypoid Set adds a car-style final drive: an 11-tooth pinion driving a 41-tooth ring gear (module 5 at
the heel), the pinion's shaft passing 30 mm below the ring's axis. On the ring gear's Placement you set
the shaft offset, shaft angle, the pinion's mean spiral angle, the hand (the usual one for the offset, or
the opposite) and the face-mill cutter radius (Auto picks one to suit). An offset of 0 gives a spiral
bevel pair. The ring gear carries the tooth size, pressure angle, face width and backlash for the set.

The teeth are generated the way they are cut. The ring gear's slots are formed by a face-mill cutter,
and the pinion is generated by rolling it against that ring gear. So the pair is conjugate by
construction: tests drive the pinion through the mesh against the ring and find no penetration, with
contact within microns along the working flanks. Generation runs in the background, taking about half a
second in a release build, and the panel shows the pitch cone, face and root angles, spiral angles and
hands, mean radii and normal module. Proportions follow Gleason practice: working depth 2 mn, clearance
0.25 mn, and the ring's addendum shared out by the ratio.

## Export

File → Export writes the selected part or the whole train as STEP (3D solids), DXF or SVG (flat
outlines, in the project's units). STEP solids are exact B-reps rather than meshes: spur and pulley
sides are the arc-fitted outline's planes and cylinders; bevel flanks are cubic B-spline cones
through the apex, with conical tip and root lands and tooth ends on the back and front cones.
Hypoid flanks and tip lands are bicubic B-spline surfaces through the generated sections. Neighbouring
faces share their edges exactly. The tooth ends are ruled patches lying close to the section cones at the
toe and heel. A test samples the STEP surfaces as a point cloud and runs the pair through the mesh: no
collision bigger than a micron.

## Timing pulleys and belts

Set a part's Type to Timing pulley to get the stock trapezoidal sections: MXL, XXL, XL, L, H, XH
and XXH. Grooves are cut from the ISO 5294 generating racks, so they match bought pulleys
(including the separate small-pulley racks for MXL and H). Stock tooth counts and belt widths
come from the same standard; picking a width sets the face width for an unflanged pulley.

Belt a pulley to another (B) and the belt's tooth count sets the centre distance. To work the
other way, type the centre distance you want: GearCraft picks the ISO 5296 stock belt that comes
closest and moves the pulley to where that belt puts it. Untick "Standard belts only" to allow any
tooth count. The panel shows the belt designation (e.g. 130XL), pitch length, wrap and the teeth
engaged on the smaller pulley, with a warning under 6.

HTD, GT2/GT3, T and AT pulleys are not in yet: their groove shapes are not fully published.

## Build and run

    cargo run --release [project.gearcraft]

Offscreen screenshot of the window (for checking layout without opening it):

    GEARCRAFT_SHOT=out.png [GEARCRAFT_SHOT_FILE=project.gearcraft] cargo test screenshot

Other `GEARCRAFT_SHOT_*` variables pick what it shows: `3D`, `BEVEL` (the Bevel Gears workspace,
adding a pair if empty), `HYPOID` (a hypoid set in 3D, waiting for its teeth), `PULLEYS`, `LIGHT`, `ANGLE=deg` (drive position), `CAM=yaw,pitch,zoom[,x,y,z]`
and `MENU=label` (click a widget first).

The interface font is IBM Plex Sans (SIL Open Font License, `assets/fonts/OFL.txt`).

Headless export of a train, or of one part on its own (axis on Z, tooth 0 on +X, at its
export origin, as File → Export writes a selected part):

    gearcraft project.gearcraft --export out.dxf     # or out.svg, out.step, out.stl
    gearcraft project.gearcraft --export gear.step --part 2   # part id or name
    gearcraft project.gearcraft --list               # the parts: id<TAB>name

CAM_Craft imports `.gearcraft` files one part at a time with `--part`.

## Shortcuts

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Ctrl+N / O / S / Shift+S | New, open, save, save as |
| Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z | Undo, redo |
| M / A | Spur Gears: add a meshing gear / a gear on the same shaft |
| M / A | Bevel Gears: add a meshing bevel / a bevel on the same shaft |
| B / P | Timing Pulleys: add a belt drive / a pulley on the same shaft |
| Del | Delete the selected gear (and anything attached to it) |
| Space | Run or pause |
| F or double-click | Fit the train to the window |
